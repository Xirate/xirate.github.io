Commute Timer – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 September 2026

Commute Timer ("the app") is made by Marcin Safiejko ("I"). This policy explains what the app does with your data. Short version: your trips stay on your phone. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads, and nothing is sold.

When location leaves your phone

A few features need an online service. They receive only what's needed for the feature, over encrypted connections. The app itself doesn't send any identifier for you:

  • Street names use Android's built-in geocoding service (provided by Google). It receives the coordinates being looked up: the spot where a traffic jam happened, or the position of the pin on the map.
  • Place search sends what you type, and the area the map shows, to Google Places to suggest addresses and places.
  • The map for choosing places is Google Maps. Google receives which part of the map is shown, as with any map app.
  • Street names for traffic jams: when Google only knows a road number (like "Droga krajowa 7"), the app asks OpenStreetMap's Overpass service for the road's name, sending the spot where the jam happened.
  • The "City grid" on shared images downloads streets from OpenStreetMap's Overpass service. It receives the rectangular area around a trip's route, when you open a trip or create a share image with the grid turned on. The result is saved on your phone, so each trip's area is requested at most once.
  • Sharing sends the image you create to the app you pick (for example Instagram), only when you tap Share.
  • Sharing a place creates a link containing that place's coordinates, name and area size, and sends it through the app you pick, only when you tap Share place. Anyone you send it to can see that location. When the link is opened, it loads a small page from GitHub Pages (xirate.github.io). GitHub receives the request, like for any website. The place is read from the link by the page itself and isn't stored anywhere.
  • Opening a Google Maps link someone shared with you: if it's a short link (maps.app.goo.gl), the app asks Google where it leads to find the coordinates. If that doesn't work, it looks up the shared text's address with the geocoding service above.

Google's map and search components (Maps SDK and Places SDK) also send Google some technical data on their own: device model and Android version, IP address, crash reports, how the map is moved and zoomed, and a pseudonymous ID that Google uses to count daily users. Google says it doesn't link this to your Google Account and uses it to run and improve its services.

These providers handle requests under their own privacy policies: Google, OpenStreetMap Foundation, GitHub.

Backups

Your data is not backed up to the cloud. If you move to a new phone using Android's phone-to-phone transfer, your trips and places come with you.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a single trip: open it and tap Delete trip.
  • Delete everything: Settings → Delete all trips, and remove places in Settings → Places.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes all of its data.

Because nothing is stored on a server, there's nothing for me to delete on my side.

Children

The app is meant for drivers and is not directed at children.
